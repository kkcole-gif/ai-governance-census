# Codebook: the specification scale

Revised November 11, 2026. This page gives the rules for scoring census documents on three levels of AI literacy. Section 6 of the user guide gives the short version. Scores are absent from the released data at this version and enter the inventory in a later release.

## 1. What is scored

Only documents with the status Verified are scored. The unit is the whole document. For an embedded document (an AI clause inside an honor code, a handbook, or an acceptable use policy), the surrounding text counts where it governs the AI provisions.

## 2. The three levels

- Conceptual: describing the system. What AI is, its types, how training and output relate, and error properties such as fabricated output treated as a property of the system.
- Structural: conditions in place before any user arrives. Training data and its norms, bias, ownership and vendor control, license terms, retention, whether user data trains the model, and labor and environmental costs. A rule about what a user is permitted to enter into a tool is operative. Data language counts as structural only where it names the system's own conditions.
- Operative: deciding, in a situation, whether to act, withhold, or refuse, and answering for the decision.

## 3. The three ratings

| Rating | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| High | The document specifies the level's content in detail a reader is able to use. |
| Medium | The document names the level or gestures at it. |
| Low | The level is absent from the document. |

## 4. Rules for the operative level

1. Pattern. High requires at least two stated rules about whether to use, withhold, or refuse AI, each with a reason given in the document's own prose, and neither dependent on the permission of an instructor or an office. One reasoned rule inside an otherwise procedural document stays at Medium.
2. Someone else decides. A document telling its reader an instructor, a unit, or an approval office decides stays at Medium. The score describes the document and its reader together.
3. Sample language. Sample or adoptable syllabus statements are offered for someone else to adopt and are not the document's own prose. A page whose reasons appear only inside sample statements stays at Medium.
4. Definitions. A definition or classification (a sentence stating a use "is plagiarism", for example) is not a stated reason.
5. System documents. A system-office rule stating reasons while handing the decision to campuses or instructors scores Medium, and its grounds are recorded.

## 5. Rules for reading

1. Reading depth. Documents are read as a member of the university community would find them. A hub or landing page reaches High only where the policy it links is read, at one click from the recorded address, and the row's Notes name the linked page.
2. Own prose. Topics named only in link titles or tab headings score as absent.
3. Compilations. A multi-tab guide or compilation scores as a whole, at the highest level any part specifies, counting only the parts read.

## 6. Addressee and grounds

- Primary addressee: the party whose conduct most operative provisions govern. A secondary addressee is recorded where a second party has provisions of its own. Values: Students, Instructors, Staff, Administrators and units, Institution-wide.
- Grounds: the kind of reason the operative rules state. Values: Compliance (law, policy, contract, data classification), Learning (what the use does to learning or to the work), Accuracy or access (reliability of output, equity of access), Both (compliance and learning together). Grounds are recorded where operative is High, and for a system-office document at Medium under rule 5.

## 7. Revision history

- October 24, 2026: scale applied to census documents.
- November 11, 2026: the pattern rule, the sample language rule, and the reading depth rule were stated after two independent readings of a 72-document test set agreed on the operative level in 60 cases and showed the earlier wording admitted single-rule documents to High.
